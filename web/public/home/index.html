the problem with portals

Most tenders leak before the deadline. On Plinths, prices stay sealed until bidders open them

one contract, every phase

Sealed by math, opened by the people who sealed it

  1. 01 / commit

    Seal a price with one hash

    Your price, a random salt and your document key are hashed with your wallet address in the browser. Only the 32-byte commitment goes on-chain. Change one digit and it no longer opens

    keccak256 · abi.encode
  2. 02 / encrypt

    Proposals locked before upload

    PDF, DOCX or text proposals are encrypted with AES-256-GCM in your browser before they reach IPFS. The key is published only when you reveal, so nobody can read the file before that

    WebCrypto · IPFS
  3. 03 / deadlines

    Deadlines live in code

    Open, Reveal and Evaluation follow block time inside the contract, with a reveal window of at least 24 hours. Late bids and early reveals revert, and the buyer can't move a deadline

    block time · revert
  4. 04 / agent

    An agent that reads, never picks

    After the reveal deadline, Plinths Agent compares the revealed proposals for the buyer and flags suspicious instructions. It never picks the winner; its report and hash are published with the award

    after reveal · buyer decides
  5. 05 / nft auctions

    The same seal, for NFTs

    Sealed-bid auctions for any ERC-721, paid in tPOTv3. Deposits are public and prices are sealed. The highest revealed price wins and settle() delivers the NFT

    ERC-721 · commit–reveal

how it works

From tender to proof in four steps

  1. 01

    Publish

    A buyer posts the scope, budget range, deadlines and an optional bid bond

  2. 02

    Seal

    Bidders commit a hash of their price, encrypt their proposal and save a backup file

  3. 03

    Reveal

    After the submission deadline, bidders reveal and the contract checks every hash

  4. 04

    Prove

    The buyer awards a revealed bid with a public reason, and anyone can verify the result
